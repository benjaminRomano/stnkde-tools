How to read these maps

Recommended starting view

Use injury crashes, synchronized yearly snapshots, 75 m spatial bandwidth, the quartic kernel, event-bearing cells and no cutoff. The narrower bandwidth reduces faint surrounding coverage. Compare 50 / 75 / 100 m to see which sections persist; this starting choice is not a statistically optimized estimator.

Smoothing and display are independent

The original event-bearing-only design is retained as the default. Full-field coverage shows estimates on roads with no raw crashes. A display cutoff changes visibility only. Bandwidth and kernel choices change the estimates. A common scale is fixed across years and is independent of coverage/cutoff.

Temporal treatment

Yearly snapshots use a temporal bandwidth of one year, so adjacent years have zero contribution. Hours use a two-hour cyclic kernel, including neighboring hours on the source lixel. Monthly snapshots use bandwidth one month. Month patterns are normalized by the observed days in each month.

Counts, intensity and risk

Headline counts include raw crash locations inside a fixed rectangle, irrespective of borough labels. Smoothed maps also use a buffer so events near the window boundary can contribute. Selected lixel counts are snapped counts and may include buffer events. Map intensities are expressed per 100 observed calendar days; they are not integrated mass or crashes per vehicle.

What remains uncertain

Current NYS roads and a planar, midpoint-distance graph are used. Historical road changes and grade-separated crossings are not modeled. Crashes without usable coordinates cannot be placed locally. COVID changed travel volume, commuting, mode mix and exposure in 2020 and the recovery period. The year gallery labels 2020 and 2021; period controls can omit both. Excluding them does not establish that later exposure returned to normal. Recorded totals also cross a reporting change in 2020, so they should not be treated as a consistent measure of all crashes. Policy attribution needs traffic volumes, treatment dates and a credible comparison design.
